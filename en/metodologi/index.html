Methodology and data sources

How We Work

An open database of Indonesian corruption cases, built on official records.

Principles

  • We only record facts stated by an official body, or reported by credible media citing official documents.
  • Every figure and every status change is footnoted to its source.
  • We don't infer. If a figure isn't stated in a source, we don't show it.
  • Automatically collected data is always reviewed by a person before it's published.

Data sources

  • Direktori Putusan Mahkamah Agung — Court verdicts: sentences, fines, restitution, confiscated assets.
  • KPK — kpk.go.id & ACCH — Announcements of suspects, detentions, seizures, and enforcement statistics.
  • e-LHKPN KPK — Published wealth declarations of state officials.
  • Kejaksaan Agung RI — Releases on cases handled by prosecutors, including major state-loss cases.
  • SIPP Pengadilan Negeri (Tipikor) — Hearing schedules and case status.
  • Indonesia Corruption Watch — Trend and verdict-monitoring reports, used for cross-checking.

Legal status

Indonesia's legal process has several stages. A person is innocent until a verdict becomes final and binding. Every profile shows the current status and its date.

Fugitive (DPO)Named a suspect and placed on the wanted list.
SuspectNamed a suspect by investigators. Not yet on trial.
On trialOn trial at the Anti-Corruption (Tipikor) Court.
Convicted (first instance)Convicted at first instance; can still appeal.
Appeal rulingHigh Court appeal ruling issued; cassation still possible.
Cassation rulingSupreme Court cassation ruling issued.
Final verdictThe verdict is final and binding.
Case review (PK) rulingCase review ruling on an already-final verdict.
AcquittedThe court found the charges not proven.
Investigation dropped (SP3)Investigators formally terminated the investigation.
DeceasedCase closed because the person died.
Presidential abolition (prosecution ended)The President granted abolition: prosecution ended without a final conviction.
Presidential amnestyThe President granted amnesty: the criminal consequences were erased.

About the rupiah figures

  • State loss — the state financial loss per the BPK or BPKP audit cited in the indictment or verdict. It is a case-level figure, not the amount any one person received.
  • Bribe / Gratuity — the amount proven or charged as received by that person.
  • Restitution — the court-ordered obligation to repay the state.
  • Foreign-currency amounts are shown as stated, with an approximate rupiah value at the rate given in the note.

Declared wealth (LHKPN)

LHKPN reports are filled in by officials themselves and published by the KPK. We show them for context; we don't verify the values, and they don't by themselves indicate wrongdoing.

Badges

The game-style badges in the yearbook are facts, not judgements. Each one is awarded automatically by the fixed rules below from sourced data, never picked by hand, and always links to the source of the fact that triggered it. Badges that imply wrongdoing only appear after a final verdict. Officials on the notice board never get badges.

  • Trillion Club

    State loss in the case of at least Rp1 trillion, with a final verdict.

    Final verdicts only.

  • Sentence Increased

    A higher court increased the prison sentence.

  • Sentence Reduced

    An appeal, cassation or case review (PK) cut the prison sentence.

  • Fugitive

    On the wanted list (DPO).

  • The Sequel

    Named in two or more corruption cases.

  • Garage Seized

    Three or more vehicles seized or confiscated, with a final verdict.

    Final verdicts only.

  • Paroled

    Released on parole.

  • Won Pretrial

    Suspect status was once annulled by a pretrial ruling.

  • Wealth Spike

    Declared LHKPN wealth rose at least 100% between two reports, with a final verdict.

    Final verdicts only.

  • Relatives in Office

    Has a recorded family tie to another public officeholder.

  • Red Card

    Political rights (the right to stand for office) revoked by the court.
